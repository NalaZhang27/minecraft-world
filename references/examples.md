# Reconstruction patterns

Use these as translation patterns, not fixed compositions.

## Portrait or animal

Keep the character or creature, its pose, defining colors, and one location cue. Place it at believable player/world scale on reconstructed terrain. Convert the surrounding location into matching block vegetation, architecture, weather, and light; remove crowds and incidental objects.

## Object or still life

Select the defining object and at most two supporting cues. Reinterpret them as world-scale landmarks, structures, resources, or terrain features rather than freestanding cube sculptures. Replace the tabletop or studio background with navigable ground, horizon, sky, and atmospheric depth.

## Architecture or travel

Keep the landmark, approach or waterway, and terrain profile. Rebuild broad facade rhythms and rooflines from structural blocks, then connect them to paths, vegetation, neighboring terrain, and a distant hazy world.

## Landscape

Keep the dominant landform, water or route, and foreground anchor. Build stepped terrain in large layers. Let vegetation and texture density diminish across the midground until distant silhouettes dissolve into haze.

## Prompt assembly

State, in order: source invariants; selected elements; world-native reconstruction; terrain and environmental integration; designed block-cloud sky; restrained source-faithful game palette; camera and lighting; foreground-to-horizon depth behavior; exclusions.

> Reconstruct the source as a believable screenshot from one expansive Minecraft-like world. Preserve [subject placement, viewpoint, background identity, major silhouettes, dominant colors, time of day, mood]. Rebuild [subjects and environment] as integrated world-native forms. Replace natural clouds with [rectangular slab clouds / stepped cloud bands] while keeping the source weather and sky colors. Consolidate the source palette into [2–4 related game-render hues] with restrained shader grading. Use large structural blocks, flat square faces, matte low-resolution surface textures, directional game light, and ambient occlusion. Keep the foreground sharp; progressively reduce detail, texture definition, contrast, and saturation into source-colored horizon haze. Avoid realistic clouds, unrequested background or palette changes, cubelet sculpture, LEGO, miniatures, shallow depth of field, bokeh, and smooth or glossy 3D.

If the result resembles a cubed photograph, redesign the subject as a world-native form and enlarge the structural scale. If it resembles a product shot, widen the camera, add navigable terrain and horizon depth, and remove lens blur.

If the sky looks photographic, replace it rather than merely pixelating it: flatten cloud volumes into a few stepped rectangular layers with crisp game-like silhouettes. If the result drifts from the source, restore its background layout, dominant color fields, time of day, and major spatial relationships before adjusting style.
